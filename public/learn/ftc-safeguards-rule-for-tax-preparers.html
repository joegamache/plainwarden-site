The FTC Safeguards Rule for tax preparers, in plain English

What the FTC Safeguards Rule requires of small tax and accounting practices, section by section, and what changes if you serve fewer than 5,000 consumers.

The FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) tells financial institutions, a category that includes paid tax preparers, how to protect customer information. Here is what each part asks of a small practice.

The nine elements (16 CFR 314.4)

  1. A Qualified Individual oversees the program. It can be an owner, an employee or an outside provider, but the firm stays responsible.
  2. A written risk assessment identifies foreseeable risks to customer information and how well current safeguards address them.
  3. Safeguards that control the risks, including:
    • access limited to people who need it, with periodic reviews;
    • an inventory of data, devices and systems;
    • encryption of customer information at rest and in transit, or an approved alternative;
    • secure development practices for any software you build;
    • multi-factor authentication for anyone accessing an information system;
    • secure disposal no later than two years after the information was last used, with some exceptions;
    • change management;
    • monitoring and logging of user activity.
  4. Testing and monitoring: continuous monitoring, or a yearly penetration test and vulnerability assessments every six months.
  5. Staff training and qualified security personnel, whether in-house or through a provider.
  6. Service provider oversight: choose capable providers, require safeguards by contract and check on them periodically.
  7. Keeping the program current as your business, technology and threats change.
  8. A written incident response plan.
  9. A yearly written report from the Qualified Individual to the owner or board.

If you serve fewer than 5,000 consumers

Section 314.6 exempts smaller institutions from the written risk assessment, the continuous monitoring or testing requirement, the written incident response plan and the yearly report. Everything else still applies, including multi-factor authentication, encryption, training and vendor oversight.

Breach notice to the FTC

Since May 2024, a notification event involving unencrypted information of at least 500 consumers must be reported to the FTC as soon as possible and no later than 30 days after discovery (16 CFR 314.4(j)). State breach laws may separately require notice to clients.

Where the IRS fits

The IRS points preparers to the same rule and publishes practical help: Publication 4557 on safeguarding taxpayer data, and Publication 5708, a sample written plan for small practices. IRS Section 7216 rules on using and disclosing tax return information apply alongside it.
