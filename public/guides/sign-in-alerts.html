Turn on sign-in alerts and activity logs

Keep a record of who signs in and what they do, get alerts for unusual sign-ins, and have someone review them.

Why it matters

Logs are how you find out a password was stolen before it becomes a breach. The FTC Safeguards Rule requires firms to monitor and log authorized users' activity and detect unauthorized access.

Steps

  1. In business email, turn on the audit log (Microsoft 365: Microsoft Purview audit; Google Workspace: Admin console reports) and alerts for suspicious sign-ins and new forwarding rules.
  2. In your tax software and cloud storage, turn on sign-in notifications and activity history where available.
  3. Turn on new-device or new-location sign-in emails for every account that offers them.
  4. Name one person to review alerts, and a backup. Check alerts the day they arrive and skim activity logs at least monthly.
  5. If you see a sign-in you can't explain, change that password, sign out all sessions, and tell the Coordinator.

You're done when

  • Activity records and alerts are on for email and tax software.
  • A named person reviews them, and reviews are noted.

References: 16 CFR 314.4(c)(8); IRS Publication 4557
