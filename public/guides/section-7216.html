Tax return information and client consent

Use tax return information only to prepare returns unless the client has given consent in the form the IRS requires.

Why it matters

Internal Revenue Code §7216 restricts how preparers use and disclose tax return information. Uses beyond preparing the return, such as marketing other services, generally need the taxpayer's written consent in a specific format. This guide is an overview only; confirm your situation with your adviser.

Steps

  1. List every way your firm uses client tax information besides preparing and filing returns: marketing other services, sharing with affiliated businesses, outsourcing, or entering it into software or AI tools.
  2. For each use, check with your adviser whether consent is needed and whether an exception applies.
  3. Where consent is needed, use consent language that meets Treasury Regulation §301.7216-3 and Revenue Procedure 2013-14, and get it signed before the use or disclosure.
  4. If any return work or information goes to a preparer or service outside the United States, ask your adviser about the additional consent rules that apply.
  5. Keep signed consents with the client file for your retention period.
  6. Train staff that client tax information isn't used for anything else without checking first.

You're done when

  • Every use beyond preparing returns is either covered by signed consent or confirmed by your adviser as not needing it.

References: IRC §7216; Treas. Reg. §301.7216-3; Rev. Proc. 2013-14
