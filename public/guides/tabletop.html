Walk through your breach checklist

Spend an hour once a year talking through a realistic incident using your plan, and fix what you find.

Why it matters

The first hours of an incident are stressful. A practice run shows whether your contact list, steps and responsibilities actually work. The FTC Safeguards Rule requires a written incident response plan.

Steps

  1. Gather the Coordinator, the backup and anyone with a role in the plan. Allow about an hour.
  2. Pick a scenario: a stolen laptop, a staff email account taken over, ransomware, or the IRS telling you fraudulent returns were filed under your EFIN.
  3. Walk through the incident response steps in your plan in order. Who notices? Who is called? What gets shut off? Who contacts the IRS Stakeholder Liaison, your insurer and affected clients?
  4. Check contact numbers in Attachment C are current.
  5. Note who must notify the FTC: an event involving unencrypted information of 500 or more consumers must be reported within 30 days of discovery. State notice rules also apply.
  6. Write down the date, who attended and what you'll change, and update the plan.

You're done when

  • A walkthrough in the past 12 months is recorded.
  • Fixes from it are made or scheduled.

References: 16 CFR 314.4(h); 16 CFR 314.4(j); IRS Publication 4557
