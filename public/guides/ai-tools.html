Use AI tools without exposing client data

Decide which AI tools may be used for client work, keep client information out of personal or free accounts, and write the rule down.

Why it matters

Free and personal AI accounts may keep what you type and use it to train their models. Entering client tax information may also be a disclosure under IRC §7216. Business AI services can offer contract terms that protect your data.

Steps

  1. List the AI tools anyone at the firm uses, including features built into other software.
  2. For each one, check whether it is a business account and whether its terms say your data isn't used for training and is protected.
  3. Approve the tools that meet those terms and add them to your vendor list. Treat them like any other provider.
  4. Write the rule: no client information in any AI tool that isn't approved, and no SSNs or full account numbers in any AI tool.
  5. Check with your adviser whether your use needs client consent under §7216.
  6. Train staff on the rule and review the list yearly.

You're done when

  • The approved AI tool list is written down.
  • Staff have been told the rule.

References: IRC §7216; 16 CFR 314.4(f)
