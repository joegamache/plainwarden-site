Back up client data and test a restore

Keep current, encrypted copies of client data somewhere separate from your computers, and prove you can restore them.

Why it matters

Ransomware and hardware failure are survivable if you have a backup that the attacker can't reach and you know it works. A backup you've never restored from is a guess.

Steps

  1. Decide what must be backed up: tax software data, client documents, email and bookkeeping files.
  2. Follow the 3-2-1 idea: three copies of your data, on two different kinds of storage, with one copy off-site or in a cloud backup service.
  3. Turn on encryption for the backup, and use two-step login on the backup service account.
  4. Keep at least one copy that ordinary computers can't change or delete (an immutable or versioned cloud backup, or a drive you disconnect).
  5. Restore a test folder to a separate location at least once a year, and after any change to how you back up. Open a few files to confirm they work.
  6. Write down the date of the test and what you restored.

You're done when

  • Backups run automatically and are encrypted.
  • A restore test in the past 12 months is recorded.

References: IRS Publication 4557; 16 CFR 314.4(c)(3)
