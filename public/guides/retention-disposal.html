Set retention periods and dispose of records

Decide how long you keep each kind of record, lock up what you keep, and securely destroy what you don't need.

Why it matters

Data you no longer hold can't be stolen. The FTC Safeguards Rule requires disposing of customer information no later than two years after it was last used to serve the customer, unless you need it for business, the law requires keeping it, or targeted disposal isn't feasible. Other rules set minimums: for example, paid preparers keep a copy or list of returns they prepared for at least three years.

Steps

  1. List what you keep: returns, source documents and workpapers, signature forms such as Form 8879, engagement letters, and records of former clients.
  2. Set a retention period for each, checking IRS, state and professional requirements with your adviser. Write it into your plan.
  3. Keep paper records in locked cabinets or a locked room, with off-site storage that is locked and access-restricted.
  4. Shred paper with a cross-cut shredder or a shredding service that gives you a certificate of destruction.
  5. Before disposing of computers, phones, drives or copiers with hard drives, wipe them with a method that meets NIST SP 800-88, or have them physically destroyed and get a certificate.
  6. Once a year, find and dispose of records past their retention period.

You're done when

  • Retention periods are written down.
  • A yearly disposal is done and recorded, with certificates where a vendor did the work.

References: 16 CFR 314.4(c)(6); IRC §6107(b); NIST SP 800-88
