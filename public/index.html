PTIN renewal reminds every preparer: you need a written data security plan.

Most small practices know they need one and keep putting it off. The IRS sample is a long fill-in-the-blanks document. Here is why it is worth doing now.

It applies to every preparer

Under the FTC Safeguards Rule, tax preparers are financial institutions, whatever their size. A one-person practice needs a written plan too.

Your e-file privileges ride on it

The IRS may treat a Safeguards Rule violation as a violation of the rules for authorized e-file providers.

It is what people ask to see after a breach

Regulators, insurers and your own clients may ask for the plan you had in place, and whether you followed it.

Questions

I'm a one-person practice. Do I really need this?
Yes. The FTC Safeguards Rule covers tax preparers of every size, and the IRS expects every PTIN holder to have a written data security plan. Solo practices get a shorter plan because they skip the questions that don't apply.
Is this legal advice?
No. PlainWarden is a tool that helps you create and maintain your plan. It doesn't certify compliance, and it doesn't replace a lawyer if you need one.
What if I have fewer than 5,000 clients?
Some written requirements don't apply to you under 16 CFR 314.6, and your plan reflects that. If you're not sure of your count, we include the full requirements to be safe.
What information do you collect?
Facts about how your practice works: software, devices, staff count, vendors. We never ask for your clients' names, Social Security numbers or financial details.
Can I edit the plan?
Yes. You get a Word file you can change, plus a PDF for your records.
What happens when Year One renews?
It renews at $199 a year. We email you 15 to 45 days before each renewal, and you can cancel online anytime.
What if it's not for me?
